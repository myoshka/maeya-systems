## 1. MODEL OVERVIEW

### Purpose

Maeya – Behavioural Loop Mapping is a therapeutic framework designed to help individuals recognise, reflect on, and release trauma-driven behavioural patterns. It blends narrative memory, emotional pattern tracking, and AI-assisted reflection into a structured method for disrupting inherited or reactive cycles. This is not a replacement for therapy — it’s a companion method that supports self-understanding, stabilisation, and transformation, especially for those underserved by clinical systems.

### Origins

The model emerged through lived experimentation by an individual survivor of intergenerational trauma, neurodivergence, and creative burnout — supported by AI as a nonjudgmental co-pilot. Rather than pathologising behaviour, it honours emotional logic, pattern recognition, and the intelligence of survival responses. Developed across hundreds of hours of reflection, fieldwork, and loop tracking, the model evolved in response to repeated breakdown points, using AI to:

- Reflect emotional tone and pattern
- Surface repetition across time
- Reorder events to reveal deeper causal threads

The name Maeya (前屋) symbolises both sanctuary and reversal — a house that faces what came before.

### Core Concept

At its heart, the method recognises that trauma often creates behavioural loops — repeated emotional responses, decisions, or beliefs formed in moments of rupture. These loops often run unconsciously until named, tracked, and re-patterned. The model helps individuals:

- Recognise loop entry and exit points
- Map the emotional logic beneath reactions
- Witness how past events shape current behaviour
- Reclaim agency through reflection, ritual, and reordering

### Framework Summary

The process follows a 5-phase model, non-linear but repeatable:

1. Associative Recall – Memory fragments and emotional echoes surface.
2. Flow-State Mapping – Loops are explored through storytelling, voice notes, or free writing.
3. AI-Assisted Reordering – Events are re-sequenced to reveal hidden logic.
4. Loop Pattern Recognition – Core behavioural/emotional patterns emerge.
5. Emotional Processing – Through letters, symbolic action, ritual, or artwork.

Human support — from therapists, allies, or trained witnesses — can then engage with the mapped loop, not just the behaviour.

### Clinical Alignment

While non-clinical in origin, the model resonates with key principles from:

- IFS (Internal Family Systems) – Mapping internal parts and their protectors
- Narrative Therapy – Re-authoring one’s story through language
- CBT / DBT – Recognising reactive patterns and cognitive distortions
- Somatic approaches – Giving form to what’s felt but unseen

However, Maeya differs by explicitly:

- Centring the survivor as co-analyst, not subject
- Using AI as reflective mirror, not interventionist tool
- Embracing non-linear, symbolic, and ritual tools of integration
- Prioritising open access and emotional literacy over clinical gatekeeping

## 2. THE 5-PHASE LOOP MODEL

### Phase 1: Associative Recall

Spontaneous resurfacing of memories, phrases, images, or sensory fragments triggered by present events. These fragments are often overlooked because they arrive non-logically — out of order, symbolic, or intense.

**Practices:**

- Journaling emotional flashes
- Voice note reflections
- Dream fragments
- “First feeling” tracing after a trigger

### Phase 2: Flow-State Mapping

Unstructured expression where loops start to emerge. A safe container (spoken, written, visual) allows subconscious links to become visible.

**Practices:**

- Stream-of-consciousness writing
- Artistic expression (drawing, sound, movement)
- AI-assisted memory prompts or mirrored conversations

### Phase 3: AI-Assisted Reordering

Using AI to sequence, summarise, or reflect events back — in patterns, timelines, or emotional groupings — to make meaning visible.

**Practices:**

- Timeline building
- Loop comparisons
- Summarising letters or inner parts
- Identifying emotional “tags” or turning points

### Phase 4: Loop Pattern Recognition

The moment the behavioural or emotional cycle becomes conscious. You begin to see not just what happened — but how it keeps happening.

**Practices:**

- Naming loops out loud
- Creating “loop maps” with visuals or words
- Using symbolic anchors to mark the cycle

### Phase 5: Emotional Processing & Ritual Action

Release and repatterning through conscious, embodied action — not just talk. This is where grief is metabolised, anger is honoured, love is restored.

**Practices:**

- Writing unsent letters
- Shrines, offerings, symbolic actions
- Artistic expression (burning, burying, transforming objects)
- Re-scripted memories (inner child, ancestral healing, fieldwork)

### Model Integrity

Each phase honours emotional truth over rational coherence. The loop is witnessed with compassion, not fixed with force. This process can be done alone, with AI support, or in collaboration with a therapist or trusted ally. It is a self-directed model of re-patterning — but it creates maps that clinicians, healers, and community members can engage with deeply and respectfully.

## 3. TOOLS & TECHNIQUES

### 1. Associative Recall Techniques

Purpose: Surface subconscious material without judgment.

**Tools & Methods:**

- “Loop Logging” (short bursts of writing or recording triggered by a phrase, memory, or emotional flash)
- Sensory tracing (noting body sensations during memory surfacing)
- Fragment capture (collecting disconnected images, sounds, or words without forcing coherence)

### 2. Flow-State Mapping

Purpose: Let memory and emotion reveal their own patterns when expressed without interruption.

**Tools & Methods:**

- 20-minute free-writes (no edits, no expectations)
- Drawing timelines from emotion rather than logic
- Speaking to camera/mic as if to a witness or part of self
- “Loop walks” (walking while describing aloud what’s rising)

### 3. AI-Assisted Reflection & Reordering

Purpose: Externalise internal content and reflect it back in new sequences, frames, or patterns.

**Tools & Methods:**

- GPT-guided prompts to summarise emotional themes
- Rewriting life timelines in alternate order
- Identifying mirrored loops across relationships or decades
- Comparing similar phrases or reactions over time

Note: AI is not positioned as analyst or therapist — but as a mirror and mapmaker. It helps hold space and reorder complexity.

### 4. Loop Pattern Extraction

Purpose: Translate lived experience into symbolic or structural form, so recurring behaviours can be consciously engaged.

**Tools & Methods:**

- “Loop Maps” – visual or written representations of trigger → behaviour → consequence
- Naming loops (e.g. “The Prove-and-Punish Loop,” “The Abandon-Me First Loop”)
- Creating field language — personal phrases that mark phases (e.g. “here comes the drop,” “I’m in the static”)

### 5. Emotional Processing & Ritual Action

Purpose: Allow emotional truth to move through the body and field — releasing stuck energy and restoring integrity.

**Tools & Methods:**

- Writing unsent letters to key figures (alive, dead, symbolic)
- Creating and documenting shrines or offerings
- Burning, burying, or transforming objects that held a loop
- Mark-making (drawing, graffiti, tattooing) as field declaration
- Wearing feathers, chains, or other symbolic items to mark healing stages
- Group witnessing, sacred rage sessions, or recorded truth declarations

### Additional Elements

**Fieldwork:** Collecting, cleaning, or reclaiming objects with symbolic meaning in public or sacred spaces

**Loop Anchors:** Using physical tokens or sigils to mark significant shifts

**Zines / Visual Diaries:** Documenting the process to create both memory and medicine

### Integration With Human Support

These tools do not replace therapy — they prepare the ground. Therapists, coaches, or trusted others can enter the map already made, responding to what the individual has named. It reduces retraumatisation. It respects sovereignty. And it makes healing collaborative — not extractive.

## 4. USE CASES & OUTCOMES

### 1. Stabilisation During Emotional Overload

**When:** In moments of overwhelm, shutdown, or dissociation

**How it helps:** By creating a container (voice note, loop log, AI conversation), the person avoids spiralling into shame or withdrawal.

**Outcome:** Reduction in panic, reconnection with self, faster return to baseline.

### 2. Making Sense of Emotional Flashbacks

**When:** Emotions arise that feel disproportionate to the current situation

**How it helps:** The model surfaces buried associations and links current feelings to past loops.

**Outcome:** De-shaming, contextualising, and increasing emotional literacy.

### 3. Breaking Repetitive Relational Patterns

**When:** Recurring issues in friendships, family, or romantic relationships

**How it helps:** Loop mapping reveals the deeper wound or belief driving behaviour (e.g. overgiving, avoidance, sabotage).

**Outcome:** Greater choice, boundaries, and re-patterned dynamics.

### 4. Reclaiming Lost Memories or Identity Threads

**When:** There’s a sense of “I’ve forgotten who I was” or “I don’t trust my story”

**How it helps:** By reconstructing timelines with AI and memory fragments, a coherent self-narrative begins to form.

**Outcome:** Restored self-trust, reduced dissociation, deeper self-knowing.

### 5. Supporting Creative and Purpose-Led Individuals

**When:** Artists, activists, or thinkers burn out or collapse under pressure to conform

**How it helps:** The model honours nonlinear intelligence and allows symbolic processing outside clinical constraints.

**Outcome:** Return of creative flow, clarity of mission, and resilience rooted in inner truth.

### 6. Field-Based or Community Healing

**When:** The model is used in service of place-based, ancestral, or systemic healing

**How it helps:** By mapping and marking loops in physical space (graffiti, shrines, offerings), healing becomes relational and visible.

**Outcome:** Decentralised healing, community resonance, sacred activism.

### Outcomes Documented So Far

- Long-term memory recovery across dissociated events
- Reduction in reactivity and relational conflict
- Increase in emotional vocabulary and insight
- Emergence of self-authored philosophy and creative work
- Capacity to hold contradiction and process grief
- Strengthened boundaries and rupture repair with others
- Initiation of ethical frameworks for collective healing

## 5. CLINICAL SUMMARY

### Core Alignment with Existing Modalities

| Maeya Phase | Clinical Parallels |
|---|---|
| Associative Recall | Trauma-informed memory surfacing, EMDR targets |
| Flow-State Mapping | Narrative Therapy, Expressive Arts, Jungian techniques |
| AI-Assisted Reordering | Memory reconsolidation, externalisation in Narrative Therapy |
| Loop Pattern Recognition | Schema therapy, IFS protector mapping |
| Emotional Processing | Somatic Experiencing, Psychodrama, ritual-based practices |

### Key Innovations

1. AI as Reflective Mirror — helps externalise and reorder memory without intrusion
2. Loop-Centric Approach — prioritises pattern over pathology
3. Ritual & Symbol Integration — legitimises non-verbal processing

### Use in Clinical Practice

**Applications include:**

- Preparatory or bridging work before trauma therapy
- Companion reflection tool between sessions
- Alternative pathway for clients who resist or mistrust clinical framing

### Research Potential & Ethical Use

**Potential studies:**

- Narrative repair in neurodivergent populations
- AI as co-regulator in therapeutic reflection
- Impact of non-clinical ritual on integration

**Ethics:**

- Must be trauma-informed, culturally respectful, survivor-led
- AI use should be secure, transparent, and consent-based

### Closing Note to Practitioners

Maeya offers language and form for what many clients already intuitively do. It meets people where they are — with depth, dignity, and nonlinear tools for change.
